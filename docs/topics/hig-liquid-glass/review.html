The simple idea

Think of it like this

Imagine reading a book while wearing frosted sunglasses.

If you place frosted glass over the book pages, the words become blurry and hard to read.

Now imagine looking at a museum display case.

The glass case holds small labels and lighting around the edges, while the sculpture sits clearly inside.

The glass frame gives structure and context without hiding the artwork.

In Apple design, your content is that sculpture.

Liquid Glass is the protective case. It belongs on navigation bars and floating controls, never on top of the content itself.

The Apple Human Interface Guidelines rest on three main pillars:

  1. Clarity:
    • Text is easy to read at every size.
    • Icons are sharp and meaningful.
    • Interactive buttons look clickable.
  2. Deference:
    • The user interface gets out of the way.
    • Content is the star of the screen.
    • Decorative chrome does not distract from user data.
  3. Depth:
    • Visual layers, shadows, and materials show how screens are organized.
    • Floating sheets and toolbars indicate where the user is inside the app.

Liquid Glass is Apple's signature spatial material.

It combines the frosted look of glass with realistic lighting and refraction.

Translucent materials let background colors peek through. This helps users stay aware of their context while looking at floating menus.

Quick check (not scored)

Why does Apple warn against stacking translucent glass surfaces on top of other glass surfaces?

Stacking multiple translucent layers muddles background details, lowers contrast, and makes text difficult to read.

In code

In UIKit, you create translucent materials using UIVisualEffectView with UIBlurEffect.

To keep text readable over changing blurred backgrounds, you add a UIVibrancyEffect.

The vibrancy effect filters the label color dynamically so words remain clear as content scrolls underneath.

Next, see how custom views must respect the Reduce Transparency accessibility setting.

When users enable this setting, your interface should replace translucent glass with a solid background color.

In SwiftUI, you add materials using the .background(.regularMaterial) or .background(.thinMaterial) view modifier.

SwiftUI handles Reduce Transparency automatically by increasing opacity on built-in materials.

Quick check (not scored)

Which accessibility setting in iOS disables blur effects and replaces materials with solid fills?

Reduce Transparency removes blur and translucency to provide maximum visual contrast.

Interview traps

Trap Using glass over core body content

Placing translucent materials over long articles or data tables makes text difficult to read.

Translucent materials belong on navigation bars, tab bars, and floating controls.

Keep content surfaces clean, opaque, and comfortable for reading.

Trap Stacking glass on glass

Placing a translucent glass modal over an already translucent glass sheet creates muddy visual artifacts.

Contrast drops sharply and icons disappear.

If a sheet is translucent, nested cards inside it must use solid, opaque colors.

Trap Ignoring the Reduce Transparency setting

Some users have low vision or motion sensitivity and enable Reduce Transparency in iOS Settings.

If you build custom blur layers that stay translucent, your app fails accessibility audits.

Always inspect UIAccessibility.isReduceTransparencyEnabled and provide high-contrast solid backgrounds.

Trap Static gray text on dynamic materials

Using static gray text on a blurred background causes legibility bugs when the background content changes.

When dark photos scroll under the glass, gray text vanishes.

Use vibrant label styles or semantic system labels that adapt dynamically to contrast shifts.

How to answer in the interview

Fresh answer

"The Human Interface Guidelines are Apple's principles: clarity, deference, and depth. Content comes first, while interface chrome recedes. Liquid Glass is Apple's translucent material used for navigation bars and sheets. It lets background colors show through to provide context, while keeping buttons easy to find."

Junior answer

"We follow Apple's deference principle by keeping the focus on user content. We use Liquid Glass and materials on navigation bars and floating sheets, never on body text. We avoid stacking glass on glass to protect contrast. We also support accessibility by falling back to solid fills when Reduce Transparency is enabled."

Senior answer

"Liquid Glass is a spatial material that creates optical hierarchy through depth and refraction. We treat materials as structural chrome that preserves background context while maintaining strict contrast standards. We pair materials with vibrancy effects to guarantee legibility across dynamic backdrops. We also verify zero contrast degradation under accessibility settings like Reduce Transparency."

Recap

  • The three HIG core themes are Clarity, Deference, and Depth.
  • Content is the star: interface chrome recedes to highlight user data.
  • Liquid Glass provides depth and contextual awareness on floating bars and sheets.
  • Never use translucent materials over long body text or core content cards.
  • Never stack glass on glass because it destroys visual contrast.
  • Use vibrancy effects to keep text readable over shifting backgrounds.
  • Respect the Reduce Transparency setting by falling back to solid opaque colors.
